# C1, C4, C5, C6 — bull and bear

The four candidates that did not reach the final. Argued properly anyway, because a
rejection without a real bull case is not a decision.

---

## C1 — Forward Deployed: the craft of the role

**Bull.** The title is having a genuine moment and nobody owns it on YouTube. The
role is aspirational, the search intent is real, and Liam is unambiguously one — the
authority is instant and requires no argument. Production cost is the lowest of any
candidate. It serves the secondary goal directly, and a channel that makes senior
engineers want the role also makes the people who *hire* for the role notice.

**Bear.** The audience cannot buy. Career content attracts job seekers with near
perfect efficiency, and every one of them dilutes the audience against the primary
goal. The bridge asset problem is unfixable: a CV template or interview guide is a
magnet for exactly the wrong person. Worse, the format's texture comes from client
engagements, so the best material is the least tellable (C-2), and the finite Tier-2
receipts get spent fast on stories that generate no revenue. There is also a subtle
positioning cost — explaining how to enter a role reads as junior-adjacent in a way
that demonstrating the work does not.

**Verdict.** Reject as content. **Retain as identity** — see ADR-004. The name is an
asset; the topic is not.

---

## C4 — Agent tooling: keeping coding agents honest

**Bull.** Much the largest and fastest-growing audience available. Liam ships the
tooling, so the authority is real and the demos are free. "Quality gates" is a
genuine counter-position against the prevailing 10x-output register, and
counter-positions travel. All receipts are Tier 1 and renewable. Cheapest format
after C5. It also feeds klasp, which is a real asset with real distribution across
three registries.

**Bear.** The audience cannot buy, and unlike C2 there is not even a leadership
minority in the mix — this is ICs almost exclusively. It sits directly adjacent to
the saturated practical-builder tier the market read told us to avoid, which means
competing on production value against people who do this full time. The bridge asset
qualifies nobody: an install is not a lead.

The specific danger is that **this candidate will produce the best-looking metrics
and the worst business outcomes.** Subscriber growth here will be visibly faster than
anywhere else, which creates continuous pressure to make more of it, which pulls the
channel toward the audience with no budget. That pressure is stronger than it looks
on paper and needs an explicit rule to resist.

**Verdict.** Occasional series, capped. Never more than one episode per season. Any
proposal to increase that allocation must cite inbound, not subscribers.

---

## C5 — Building Reality AI in public

**Bull.** Cheapest format on the list. Authentic, and authenticity is the scarce good
in the post-slop market. Compounds if the business succeeds visibly.

**Bear.** No search intent exists for an unknown person's business update, and
YouTube's recommendation system will not manufacture it. The format demands current
numbers that cannot be published. Client work is the substance of the business, so an
honest account either breaches confidentiality or is hollowed out into something
nobody wants to watch — there is no stable middle. The bridge asset is nothing.

**Verdict.** Reject outright. This material belongs on LinkedIn, where the network
already exists and the format actually works. It should not consume a video slot.

---

## C6 — Enterprise AI interviews

**Bull.** The strongest business logic of any candidate: the interview *is* a
business development conversation, and the relationship formed in booking it is worth
more than the published video. Both the guest and the audience can buy. The media
network (R15) is a genuine unlock that competitors cannot replicate. Proven format at
the top end of the market.

**Bear.** 12–20 hours per episode against an 8-hour budget — a direct violation of
C-1, and the overrun is paid out of billable time. Good guests require an existing
audience, which does not yet exist, so the precondition fails. Guest NDA constraints
are tighter than Liam's and add an approval loop that can kill an episode after all
the work is done. Search discovery is poor: nobody searches for an interview with
someone they have not heard of.

**Verdict.** Defer to year two; revisit at the season 3 or 4 gate.

**But extract the business logic now.** The valuable part is the conversation, not
the video. Start having these conversations immediately, unrecorded and unpublished
— all of the business development value at none of the production cost. That is not
a content strategy, it is just calling people, and it should not wait for a channel.
