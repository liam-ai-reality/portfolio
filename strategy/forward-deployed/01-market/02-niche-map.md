# Niche map

Where the AI-adjacent YouTube audience currently sits, and where the space is.

## The tiers

| Tier | Example content | Audience size | Buyer proximity | Competition | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Research explainers** | Paper breakdowns, architecture deep-dives | Large | Low | High, and requires credentials Liam does not have | Out |
| **Practical builders** | "Build an agent with n8n", tool tutorials | Very large | Low | Saturated, slop-adjacent | Out |
| **Tool reviews / news** | "Top 10 AI tools", model launch reactions | Very large | Very low | Saturated, commoditised | Out |
| **Industry analysis** | Market commentary, strategy takes | Medium | Medium | Medium; crowded with non-practitioners | Weak fit — opinion without receipts |
| **Long-form interviews** | Lex-style multi-hour conversations | Large | High | Low but requires existing audience | Deferred (see C6) |
| **Production engineering** | What breaks when AI meets a real company | Small | **High** | **Low** | **Target** |
| **Regulated / compliance AI** | EU AI Act, auditability, governance | Very small | **Very high** | **Near zero** | **Target** |

## The whitespace, stated precisely

The market has enormous coverage of *building* and almost none of *deploying*. The
distinction matters commercially: people who want to build watch tutorials and pay
nothing; people who need to deploy have a budget, a deadline and usually a regulator.

Three specific empty spaces:

1. **The gap between a demo and a system.** Multi-tenancy, authz, cost at scale,
   observability, audit trails, rollback. Universally needed, almost never filmed.
2. **EU AI Act as engineering, not law.** Abundant legal commentary, almost no
   content from someone who has *shipped* classification, FRIA workflow, Annex IV
   documentation and post-market monitoring as software. The August 2026 GPAI
   obligations put a clock on this.
3. **Organisational failure modes.** Pilots die in procurement, security review and
   user adoption far more often than in engineering. Nobody films this because
   almost no creator has been in the room.

## Adjacent creators to study, not imitate

Populate `competitor-teardowns/` with 6–8 teardowns before season 2. Study for
format and packaging, never topic — the topic overlap is where the trap is. Priority
targets: the largest practical-builder channel in the agent space, one enterprise
architecture channel, one compliance/GRC channel if one exists at all, and one
technical creator with a small audience but a high-value B2B business behind it.

That last category is the real comparable. The metric to extract from them is not
subscribers — it is what they put in the description and what the bridge asset is.
