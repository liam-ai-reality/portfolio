# Market read — July 2026

Four dynamics that determine what is worth making. Sources at the bottom.

## 1. The slop crackdown is a tailwind, not a threat

YouTube terminated 16 channels with a combined 35M subscribers and 4.7B lifetime
views in January 2026 under the inauthentic-content policy. Roughly 1 in 5 Shorts
recommended to a fresh account is mass-produced AI video — a Kapwing study of 15,000
trending channels found 104 of the first 500 recommendations to a new account were
slop (20.8%). Neal Mohan used the phrase "AI slop" in his own January 2026 letter.

The stated survival criterion is whether a viewer can tell a human made creative
decisions. Channels that survived share a human voice, original value and genuine
engagement.

**What this means here:** face on camera, a real terminal, a real failing test, a
real cost model — these were table stakes eighteen months ago and are a moat now.
The single cheapest differentiator available is being visibly, checkably a
practitioner. Note the collateral damage though: faceless human creators have been
caught in the purge, which independently supports the face-on-camera decision.

## 2. The tutorial tier is saturated and commoditised

The "build an AI agent in 10 minutes" / n8n-workflow lane is owned — Nate Herk went
from zero to hundreds of thousands of subscribers in under two years on exactly this
— and a model can now produce a passable imitation of it. Over a million channels
use AI creation tools daily.

**What this means here:** do not enter this lane. It is high-volume, high-competition,
and its audience is the least likely to have a budget. Any candidate whose core loop
is "teach the viewer to build the thing" inherits this problem.

## 3. Format economics: long-form is the product, Shorts are plumbing

- Long-form: 55% of net ad revenue to the creator.
- Shorts: 45% of a *pooled* regional share; typical RPM $0.01–0.07. A million
  engaged Shorts views is $10–70 for most channels.
- The Shorts algorithm is fully separate from long-form and optimises swipe-through,
  loop rate and early engagement.
- Creators posting both formats grow subscribers ~3x faster than single-format channels.

**What this means here:** long-form carries all the authority and all the intent.
Shorts exist only as a discovery feeder and must be **cut from** long-form, never
authored separately — authoring them separately would violate constraint C-1 for
approximately zero return.

## 4. B2B inverts the view-count logic

For a consulting business the relevant keywords are long, specific and low-volume,
but each view is worth vastly more than a consumer view. A video ranking for a term
like "ServiceNow implementation roadmap for mid-market" at 200 views a month can
produce one qualified lead that closes six figures. B2B buyers watch several videos
before making contact, and find them through search before they know the creator.

The conversion mechanic that works is a bridge asset — a template, framework PDF,
checklist or teardown — not "book a demo".

**What this means here:** topic selection should optimise for *buyer proximity*,
not volume. This is the single biggest departure from generic YouTube advice and it
is weighted accordingly in the stage 03 rubric.

## The gap this leaves

Everyone teaches how to **build** an AI demo. Almost nobody shows what happens when
one meets a real organisation: multi-tenancy, authorisation, cost at ten thousand
users, an audit trail, a regulator, a procurement cycle, and an end user who does not
want the tool. That is the whole of Liam's working life and it is under-served.
