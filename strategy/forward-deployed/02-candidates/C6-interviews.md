# C6 — Enterprise AI interviews

## Premise
Long-form conversations with the people actually deploying AI inside large
organisations — engineering leaders, platform owners, compliance heads. Not founders
and not researchers: operators. Liam's media-sector network (R15) is the unlock.

## Core loop
One guest, 45–75 minutes, on what they actually shipped and what it cost them.

## Who watches
Peers of the guest. Small, senior, extremely well qualified.

## Can they buy?
**Yes, and so can the guest.** This is the highest-value property of the format:
the interview *is* a business development conversation, and the relationship formed
in booking and recording it is worth more than the published video.

## Receipts spent
R15 as network access, R7/R8 as the credibility that makes senior people say yes.

## NDA route
A and C, plus the guest's own constraints — which are usually tighter than Liam's
and require pre-agreed boundaries and, realistically, guest approval before publishing.

## Production cost
**Very high.** 12–20 hours all-in per episode: outreach, scheduling, prep, recording,
a long edit, and the approval loop. Roughly three times the target budget and a
direct violation of constraint C-1.

## Discovery mechanism
Guest audience and referral. Poor search discovery — nobody searches for an interview
with a person they have not heard of.

## Bridge asset
The conversation itself.

## Kill condition
If three outreach attempts to target guests go unanswered, the audience precondition
has not been met and the format is premature.

## Verdict
**Defer to year two.** The format is right and the business logic is excellent, but
it needs an audience before good guests accept, and it cannot be afforded until the
production process is efficient. Revisit at the season 3 or 4 review gate.

A cheaper interim: do these as *unpublished* conversations. All of the business
development value, none of the production cost. That is just calling people, and it
should start immediately regardless of what the channel does.
