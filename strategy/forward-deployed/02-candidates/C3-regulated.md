# C3 — Regulated: the EU AI Act as engineering

## Premise
The EU AI Act treated as a software problem rather than a legal one. Risk
classification under Articles 5/6 and Annex III, the FRIA workflow, Annex IV
technical documentation, post-market monitoring — each shown as something you build
and run, not something you read about. There is abundant legal commentary and almost
no content from someone who has shipped this as working software.

## Core loop
Take one obligation. Explain what it actually requires in engineering terms. Build
or demonstrate the artefact that satisfies it. Give the artefact away.

## Who watches
CTOs and Heads of AI at European companies with a deadline. Compliance and GRC leads
who have been handed AI oversight and do not have an engineer who understands it.
Product owners at companies selling into the EU. Very small audience, unusually
concentrated.

## Can they buy?
**Yes — the highest buyer proximity on this list.** These viewers arrive with a
deadline, a budget, and no internal capability. That combination is the definition
of a qualified lead.

## Receipts spent
R12, the seven-tool suite. This is the single most differentiating receipt in the
inventory and this candidate is the only one that spends it properly. R13 secondary.

## NDA route
A and C. The regulation is public; the implementation patterns are reconstructable
on synthetic data. The suite itself was built for a client, so the *product* cannot
be shown — but the *approach* can be rebuilt from scratch in public, and arguably
should be, as it would become an OSS asset in its own right.

## Production cost
Medium. 5–7 hours. Low build cost where artefacts already exist conceptually, but
high scripting cost — regulatory precision is unforgiving and an error here damages
credibility with exactly the audience that matters.

## Discovery mechanism
Search, almost entirely. Low volume, very high intent, near-zero competition. The
August 2026 GPAI obligations create a demand spike with a date attached.

## Bridge asset
**Exceptional.** An Annex IV technical documentation template, a risk-classification
decision tree, a FRIA worksheet. Anyone who downloads one of these has a live
compliance obligation and no way to meet it. This is the strongest bridge asset
available anywhere in this repo.

## Kill condition
If, after four episodes, view counts are near zero *and* no downloads convert to
conversations, the intent hypothesis is wrong and the niche is genuinely empty
rather than under-served. Note that low views alone does **not** kill this — low
views are expected and priced in.

## Risk to manage
Two. First, this is work rather than fun, and as a sole format it burns out fast.
Second, accuracy: a wrong claim about a legal obligation is worse than no video.
Every episode needs a stated "I am an engineer, not your lawyer" boundary, and the
content must stay on the engineering side of it.
